# Creative direction: decisions before effects

Load for a new film, an open creative brief, or a weak/repetitive result. A render passing technical checks is not evidence of taste. The agent directs; the toolkit supplies a vocabulary and executable foundations.

## Find the film, not the template

Write the audience, promise, evidence and emotional change in one sentence each. Identify the best available visual before writing a row of headlines. For a redesign or broad request, sketch two or three distinct territories, select the strongest and continue. Distinct means different assets, framing, camera/edit grammar and sound, not just colors. A precise trim/caption request does not need a new campaign concept.

Useful territories include a tactile product film (macro/detail/hero), a proof-first screen film (result/action/consequence), a human micro-story (question/obstacle/answer), an editorial collage (photography/type/cutouts), and an abstract spatial metaphor (a real relationship made visible). Avoid making every idea a dashboard in a rounded rectangle.

For a spatial or asset-heavy concept, make a few keyframes, a look board or a proxy animatic before a long render or paid take set. Judge composition and asset fit while changes are cheap.

Write a compact direction note: dominant subject; palette/material/light; type hierarchy; camera behavior; edit rhythm; sound character; opening image; proof hold; ending. Say what will be omitted. Reference an observable mechanism from a source—occlusion, match movement, delayed sound, constrained perspective—not a vague request to copy a famous creator's style.

## Reference breakdown

When references are available, inspect the actual video or a contact sheet; a repository README is not a visual analysis. Log time range, subject, shot size, focal/depth cues, camera/object motion, transition cause, typography, asset type and sound event. Separate observations from hypotheses. Extract the mechanism; create new assets and choreography.

Use `scout <video> --out <new-directory>` to sample supplied media before selecting shots. A sparse sheet can miss important actions; inspect additional frames around cuts and gestures. `ideas list` indexes the curated shot recipes; `ideas <id>` loads only one. These recipes suggest mechanisms, not a compulsory storyboard.

## A hierarchy that survives motion

Give each beat one dominant subject and one understandable change. Supporting texture, captions and sound should make that change easier to follow. Choose real footage, exact product evidence, a cast 3D object, typography or a diagram by what the claim needs. A generic spinning object is not product evidence.

Compose the whole frame. Backgrounds and world geometry can fill the canvas; protect critical text and faces from platform UI. Do not squeeze all art into a narrow upper band because the caption has a safe box. Design negative space intentionally, then check the actual phone crop.

Contrast carries energy: wide/detail, quiet/impact, dense/open, human/product, travel/settle. Repeating a new headline in the same stage every second is still a slide deck. A fast camera should arrive somewhere that changes the story; a cut may be clearer than a long tour.

## Before/after decisions

| Weak default | Better direction when appropriate |
|---|---|
| Logo intro followed by feature cards | Open on a result, conflict or striking object; identify the brand on a meaningful beat |
| Tiny whole website in a browser frame | Show one readable action at useful scale; preserve enough context to identify the real product |
| Constant orbit, shake and spring | One motivated camera move, stable evidence, selective impacts |
| Every word gets an animation and sound | Emphasize the decisive phrase; let speech and action lead |
| Stock footage vaguely matching a noun | Cast a specific asset that demonstrates cause, scale, context or consequence |
| Empty pause covered with unrelated confetti | Anticipation, reaction, process evidence, a visual answer or an intentional silence |
| New palette on the old edit | Change staging, asset language, timing and transitions |

## Critique the result, not the checklist

Review at intended display size and with sound off. Can a viewer name the subject immediately? Does each shot advance the promise? Is the proof readable? Is the moving camera revealing depth or just making text harder to read? Do the best assets carry the film, or do generic boxes conceal their absence? Is the last image complete rather than a held face under a fading outro?

Record a concrete defect and a change, not “make it premium.” For example: “the product is 18% of the portrait frame; cut to a three-quarter close-up and hold before the next noun.” Reinspect the changed neighborhood. Technical validation, editorial judgment and measured audience results are separate claims.
