# Short-form direction for Instagram, YouTube and X

Choose a destination before composing. These are editorial starting points; they do not predict distribution or replace current account/upload rules. Recheck the live destination and account at publishing time. Do not promise “viral” results from an effect count.

| Destination | Creative priority | Starting composition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Immediate visual identity, a clear human/product moment and a satisfying transformation | 9:16, intentional full-frame art, concise readable captions | Check feed/profile cover crop and actual UI; keep key faces, evidence and text clear |
| YouTube Shorts | A specific question/promise, visible progress and a satisfying answer | 9:16 is the native creative starting point; title and opening must describe the same promise | Give technical explanations enough proof time; make a loop semantically honest rather than chopping the answer |
| X feed video | An idea/result understandable as a poster and on mute, then substantive proof | Choose 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 or 9:16 for the actual audience/feed context | Recompose evidence and captions; don't assume the vertical master is the best framing for dense code/UI |

## Hook and reading load

An opening can be a result before the process, a meaningful contradiction, a striking product detail, a demonstrated problem or a human question. Avoid a generic “stop scrolling” line with no payoff. Let the first frame identify the subject, while the first beat creates a reason to continue.

Treat pace as new understandable information plus motion contrast. Fast cuts can surround a stable proof hold. A technical action may need longer than a slogan. Keep captions short enough to read without competing with a different animated headline. UI copy, code and data need larger framing or fewer concurrent facts.

## One film, deliberately conformed versions

Create a shared story/asset plan, then adjust framing, line lengths, evidence scale, caption position and CTA for each format. Full-bleed footage/worlds can occupy the canvas; safe placement applies to critical content, not a rule to shrink every visual into the same box. Check the destination overlay if available. The existing `reels` safe-area preset is a conservative project assumption, not a universal official mask; `clean` provides another composition starting point. Neither detects faces.

Use a clean or selected poster frame that communicates the topic without player controls. On X, write the post and thumbnail to complement the film rather than duplicating a paragraph of subtitles. On Shorts/Reels, align the opening with the promised answer. End with one useful action when a CTA is appropriate; a closing brand card is optional, not compulsory for an editorial short.

## Current technical facts vs creative choices

Checked 2026-10-20: YouTube's help describes square/vertical uploads up to three minutes as eligible Shorts subject to channel/date conditions. This is eligibility, not an editorial target. X's ordinary web-upload help lists up to 140 seconds/512 MB for non-Premium accounts, with separate Premium conditions. Meta's Reels advertising guidance emphasizes vertical 9:16, audio and safe placement; ad guidance does not establish an organic-ranking formula.

A practical local delivery master is H.264 MP4 with AAC when audio exists, original timeline FPS and a dimension/profile supported by the destination. Keep a high-quality editable master and caption sidecar. For this toolkit's 24/30/60 FPS choices, choose from actual capture/motion needs rather than inventing detail through upsampling. Rights to app-library music may not transfer to an exported cross-platform film.

Check current duration/codec/file-size rules, rights, cover crop, caption spelling, sound-off comprehension, audio and final-frame behavior before an authorized upload. Native Video Forge prepares files; it does not publish.
