# Brand-led footage edits and product evidence

Use when a talking-head edit names a real company/product, or the user asks for its logo, website, product visuals or visual theme. Make a small editorial plan before picking effects; the following are decisions to resolve, not a fixed template or demand for extra user approval.

## Decide what the edit must communicate

Identify the audience, one takeaway and the next action. Preserve the user's approved source order, complete speech and factual qualifiers. Map each spoken claim to a useful visual: a real screenshot, a brief diagram, a product action, or the speaker. Use pauses for evidence/payoff rather than leaving a row of unrelated stickers. Choose pace from speech and reading load, not an arbitrary effect quota.

## Inspect the brand and source

Use the official site and observed logo/image URLs. Record source URLs, capture dates/states and applicable asset terms separately from original code/music. Read the actual relevant product material before illustrating an integration or feature. Do not copy a publisher's performance claim into a new measured-results assertion.

Inspect logo variants, actual colors, typography and UI density. Respect explicit user constraints such as no added gradients; an authentic captured page may contain its own artwork. Preserve logo proportions and contrast. Use a neutral backing when a logo conflicts with busy footage.

Distinguish live UI capture, a screenshot with an illustrative pointer, and a constructed workflow diagram. A pointer click in a video is not proof that an account action, booking or integration succeeded. Don't invent private dashboard states or expose account data. Use public evidence or clearly labeled illustrations when no account is available.

## Choreograph and protect the message

Use native `overlays` for brand reveals, call motion, browser/screenshot inserts, workflow steps, labels and closing CTAs. Read [overlay contract](../../../docs/project-format.md#production-overlays-v050). Choose where each overlay belongs from the actual face, hands, evidence and caption positions; `offsetY` supports deliberate placement and does not track faces.

Screenshot `aspectRatio`, fit/focus and cursor coordinates must describe the same image. Focus on a readable section rather than shrinking a desktop dashboard into a phone. Give evidence a clear hold. Pointer travel, a highlight or click ripple should direct attention; pair important changes with restrained sound accents. Keep captions above full-frame inserts and preserve measured word timing.

Use motion contrast: an immediate human hook, one conceptual transformation, real product evidence, a return to the speaker and a readable CTA. Vary staging and scale; do not bury every shot under simultaneous effects. Keep the source resolution limitation visible in the production notes; an upscale does not recover detail.

## Verify and deliver

Use `review`, then `preview` for entry/peak/exit frames, including the first and last beats, screenshot crops, pointer landing points, faces, captions and overlay intersections. Prepared footage without cuts supports preview; otherwise render the edit first. Check the final encoded output as well. Verify source order, complete transcript, audio continuity, voice/music level and final decoding separately from subjective visual review.

Preserve the native project, overlay timings, source-to-claim/asset map, screenshots/logos, captions and credits. Keep private footage and third-party brand assets outside the public plugin repository. A local chapter-based review player may add actual seeking and links; label an MP4's pointer animation as an illustrative product moment, not an interactive live application.
