# Cast assets before decorating the edit

Use when an idea needs footage, photography, product captures, 3D objects, materials, typography, music or generated imagery. An asset is a narrative choice, not a box to fill. A large library of links is not a prepared asset pack.

## Map each beat to a visual role

Identify hero, evidence, context, texture, transition surface and sound. Prefer supplied authentic material for product facts. Capture one relevant action at a readable scale instead of guessing a dashboard. Ask what a shot demonstrates: scale, friction, detail, outcome, environment, relationship or emotion. Reject an otherwise attractive asset when it does not help that role.

For 3D, choose silhouette, units, orientation, topology, material maps, texture resolution and lighting to suit the camera path. A model that works as a distant set piece may fail in a macro hero. Check animation/rigging and external textures before promising a move. Procedural geometry is useful for abstract ideas; do not present it as a real product scan.

## Search and inspect with a concrete brief

Search by subject + action/view + material/light + format + rights need, for example “desk microphone three-quarter glTF neutral studio” rather than “cool 3D assets.” Review a contact sheet or turnaround, then the actual selected file. Check dimensions, compression, frame rate, alpha/color interpretation, texture dependencies and readable branding. For reference videos, analyze mechanisms instead of copying entire edits or branding.

Start from the curated [asset source index](../../video-forge/assets/asset-sources.json). It records discovery URLs and license verification scope, not a blanket right to every result. Poly Haven is useful for CC0 models/materials/HDRIs; Kenney is useful for stylized game assets; original vectors/procedural meshes are reliable for exact abstract forms. Keep photography/video, fonts, sound and brand assets on their own rights records.

Do not download a whole repository of media just because its code is MIT/Apache. The inspected Shotcraft repository explicitly separates its audio terms and renderer dependency from its code. Check per-asset licenses and attribution even on an open-source project. Keep required notices with reused code; do not import GPL editor code into this MIT toolkit when only its workflow ideas are needed.

## Build a consistent kit

Choose a coherent family of camera angle, light direction, material roughness, palette, texture scale and type. A random mix of glossy stock 3D, hand-drawn icons and unrelated photos needs an intentional collage concept to look designed. Match scale and shadow behavior across assets. Use exact local logo vectors/screenshots for brands; keep text and numerical evidence outside a generative model when precision matters.

Retain the chosen asset, source URL, retrieval date, original license/notice, modifications and credit in the manifest. Record limitations: upscaling, missing textures, editorial-only use, flattening or illustrative substitutes. A wishlist entry remains missing until the actual file is local and inspected.

## Generate a missing shot deliberately

Read [shot-generation direction](generation-direction.md). Start with a look/continuity reference and one camera/action idea per shot. An animatic with proxy geometry can validate framing and timing before paid generation. Keep provider job IDs, exact prompts/inputs, selected take and rejected-take reasons. Do not equate endpoint acceptance with a finished usable shot.

Use local/code-native rendering for exact logos, code, data, diagrams and editable typography. Use AI for material that benefits from visual invention and tolerates variation. Cast, evaluate, replace and conform generated shots as editorial assets; don't hide their failure with more overlays.
